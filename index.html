Who We Are

Some of us took those 2 a.m. wing bullas a little too seriously.

SHIFT began with the kind of conversations every IITK wing has. Politics, startups, AI, villages, culture, climate, public policy. The difference was that we kept coming back to the same questions instead of leaving them as just another wing bakchodi.

We meet to understand questions that matter. Sometimes that means reading a book together, breaking down a policy, inviting someone who's worked on the problem, or simply arguing until everyone has a clearer position. The goal isn't to agree. It's to think better.

When discussions leave us with questions we can't answer from campus, we go looking. We visit people, places, and organisations, learn from those closest to the problem, and occasionally turn what we've learnt into projects of our own. Good conversations deserve better endings than another chai run.

What SHIFT usually looks like
  1. Pick a real question. One that's worth spending time on.
  2. Understand it properly. Discussions, readings, guest speakers, and different perspectives.
  3. Build your own view. Challenge assumptions, defend ideas, and change your mind if you have to.
  4. Go beyond the discussion. Field visits, conversations, or projects whenever the question calls for it.

Beyond the closed rooms

Sometimes the best way to understand is to step outside.

Good discussions are only the beginning. Any group can get comfortable with its own ideas if they're never challenged. So depending on what we're exploring, we read together, invite people who've lived the problem, visit places where it's unfolding, or try building something ourselves.
